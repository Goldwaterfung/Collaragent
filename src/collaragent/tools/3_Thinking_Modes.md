# 3. Thinking Modes

*The overarching styles or directions of thought. These describe the general "flavor" or "gear" of how you are processing information. They can be automatic or intentional.*

### Analytical Thinking
A methodical, step-by-step approach that involves breaking down complex wholes into smaller, manageable parts. It helps clearly isolate the root cause and evaluate components individually.

### Creative (Divergent) Thinking
Generating new, innovative ideas by looking at things from multiple unconventional perspectives and exploring multiple potential solutions rather than seeking one correct answer.

### Convergent Thinking
Synthesizing, organizing, and analyzing different pieces of information to find a single, definitive "best" solution to a problem.

### Critical Thinking
Carefully evaluating information, arguments, biases, and evidence to form a well-reasoned, objective judgment.

### Concrete Thinking
Focusing on the practical, literal aspects of what is immediately present. It relies on the facts at hand.

### Abstract Thinking
Focusing on theories, conceptual ideas, and relationships that aren't physically present. It allows for metaphorical understanding (e.g., concepts of justice or probability).
